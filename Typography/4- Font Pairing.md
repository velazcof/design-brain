
Font pairing is about choosing typefaces that feel **different enough to create hierarchy, but related enough to feel intentional together**.

✔️ A good pairing gives each typeface a clear role and keeps the overall mood cohesive.

❌ A bad pairing either looks too similar to create contrast, or so different that the fonts feel unrelated.


### Common Pairing Strategies

|Pairing|Effect|Typical Use|
|---|---|---|
|**Serif heading + sans-serif body**|Strong contrast with good readability|Editorial, portfolios, branded sites|
|**Sans-serif heading + serif body**|Modern structure with traditional reading feel|Long-form content, documentation|
|**Two related sans-serifs**|Clean and restrained|Product UI, dashboards|
|**Expressive display + neutral body**|Strong personality without hurting readability|Hero sections, marketing pages|

Keep expressive fonts mostly for larger text. Body text should prioritize clarity and sustained reading.


### What Makes Two Fonts Work Together

Look for some shared characteristics even when the fonts are visually different:

- Similar **x-height** and proportions
    
- Compatible **stroke weight**
    
- Similar openness in counters and apertures
    
- A related mood or design tradition
    
- Weights that feel visually balanced

> **They should feel like siblings — not twins, and not strangers.**


### Choosing Roles

Usually, keep the system simple:

```
Display / Heading → personality and emphasis
Body              → readability and neutrality
Mono (optional)   → code or technical data
```

Two typefaces are usually enough. Adding more increases visual noise and makes the system harder to maintain.


### Testing a Pair

Do not judge fonts only from specimen pages.

Test using:

- real headings and paragraphs
- the actual sizes you plan to use
- buttons, labels, and navigation
- light and dark themes
- mobile and desktop layouts

The pairing needs to work inside the actual interface, not just look nice beside each other.


### Performance

Each additional font family adds loading cost.

Prefer:

- variable fonts when available
- only the weights/styles you actually use
- modern `.woff2` files
- avoiding unnecessary third or fourth typefaces


### Common Mistakes

- **Pairing fonts that are too similar.**  
    The hierarchy becomes weak.
    
- **Pairing fonts with completely different personalities.**  
    The design feels inconsistent.
    
- **Choosing body fonts for personality over readability.**  
    Body text has to work repeatedly at small sizes.
    
- **Adding too many families.**  
    More fonts rarely means better typography.
    
- **Choosing fonts in isolation.**  
    Always test them with real content and your actual layout.
